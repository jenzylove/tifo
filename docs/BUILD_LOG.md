## 10 Oct 2026: the money rules (Phase B)
- `TifoPromo`: the offer steps down when a side's budget is used up (current rate, then the floor, then no rebate), so "free if your team wins" gives exactly as many free spots as the budget covers and a sale is never refused for budget. A buyer passes the lowest offer they accept and is refused (`OfferGone`) if it has run out. A draw pays every buyer a thank you (`drawBps`); the reserve covers the largest of three bills. An order reference is charged once (`DuplicateOrder`).
- `TifoAccount` (new): the seller's account. Sales land in it. The owner sets a weekly rule (a base amount plus a share of the last 7 days of sales) and a limit per drop. Inside the rule the agent opens drops by itself; outside it the agent can only propose and the owner decides. Only the owner can withdraw. Created through `TifoFactory.createAccount`.
- Core math mirrors the contract. Tests: 13 contract tests (invariant: 256 runs, 15,360 calls, reserve always equals the largest of the three bills and never passes the budget) and 12 math tests (2,000 lopsided promos settled on all four outcomes).
- Web: the seller form offers "Free if your team wins" or "Money back for everyone"; the drop page shows free spots left per side and the draw thank you; checkout shows the offer the order will really get.
- Read "Agents and Ledgers in 2026" (the hackathon's required reading) and mapped each control to the code in the README. It led to the duplicate order guard.
- Testnet factory `0x5E7aCE925Fb504C88ECd7b2Bb86b77af0EEaD6Bd` (same agent and operator as before). Verified live with `scripts/e2e-money.mts` (drop `0x64c55fd2bafdee4e939f2ef1fad1021ef6275b15`, 13 checks) and `scripts/e2e-delivery.mts` (11 checks).
- Not yet built: the screen where a seller sets the weekly rule and approves proposals, and the agent opening drops by itself from the scheduler. That is Phase C.

## 10 Oct 2026: the agent that decides (Phase C)
- `lib/planner.ts`: the agent's own decision. Given the seller's past drops, their rule and the coming fixtures with live odds, the model decides whether to run a drop, on which match, in which shape and with what budget, and explains it. Everything after that is code: the fixture must be one it was given, the discount is lowered until it is under the seller's profit per sale or the drop is refused, offers come from the core math, and the plan is marked "open" only if the budget is inside both limits of the seller's rule. Otherwise it is a proposal.
- `lib/autopilot.ts` and `autopilotTick` in `lib/agent.ts`: once every six hours per seller the scheduler asks for a plan. Inside the rule the agent calls `openDeal` on the seller's account and writes its reason on the new drop; if the contract answers `OverLimit` it falls back to `propose`. One drop at a time, and never while a proposal is waiting.
- Autopilot tab: the seller turns it on by setting a weekly amount, a share of last week's sales and a limit per drop. It shows what is left this week, what the manager plans next and why, proposals to approve or decline, and the account balance with a button to move it to the wallet.
- With autopilot on, drops the seller makes by hand open through the account too, so sales land in one place and count toward the week.
- Scorecard: each drop shows whether the manager opened it and whether the contract's balance matches the book to the cent.
- Verified with `scripts/plan-check.mts` against 25 real fixtures and the live model (16 checks): with room in the rule it chose Real Madrid v Villarreal at 15% back and a $40 budget, citing the last drop's 9 to 1 split; with the week used up it sent a proposal instead of acting; on a $1 margin it refused and said why.
- Verified through the running app on testnet: `/api/manager` returns the test seller's account (rule, allowance, three drops, all matching the chain) and `/api/autopilot` declines to plan while a drop is running.
- Not yet verified: the agent sending `openDeal` or `propose` from the scheduler, because its key lives only in Vercel, and the Autopilot tab by hand, because it sits behind Face ID. Both need a deployment.

## 15 Oct 2026: release audit fixes, live on Arc mainnet and testnet
A release audit found four money issues in the contracts and one in digital delivery. All are fixed and live.
- Digital delivery: a sealed product is now bound to the seller who sealed it, and opens only for an order that paid at least one unit's price. Before, a stranger could copy the sealed text into their own drop, or pay a fraction of a cent, and open it.
- `TifoPromo`: every order pays at least the price of one unit (`minAmount`), so booking slots and the "switch the offer on" count cannot be taken with dust. A refund the token refuses is kept for that buyer to claim instead of stopping everyone else's. 58 contract tests, including a fuzz run over physical drops.
- Agent: each scheduler pass sends at most 30 transactions, drops holding the most money first, and with under 0.5 USDC of gas left it only works on drops holding money. Public boards list new drops only when they were opened through a Tifo account; `HIDDEN_DROPS` takes spam off the site.
